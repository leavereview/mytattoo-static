## What makes tattoo booking software different

General booking tools (Calendly, Acuity, salon software) are good at calendars and card payments. They were built for services that are short, repeatable and priced in advance. Tattooing is none of those things:

- **Every piece is quoted.** You need to see the idea, size, placement and references before you can price it or say how long it will take.
- **Sessions are long and vary.** A small flash piece might take an hour; a back piece is several full days. Fixed 30-minute slots do not fit.
- **Deposits are standard.** Most artists take a deposit before they hold a date, and need a clear policy on when it is kept or refunded.
- **Consent forms are part of the job.** Date of birth, health questions and the ID you checked all belong with the client's record.
- **Clients arrive from Instagram.** They tap a link in your bio on a phone. Asking them to create an account loses a share of them.

Software that handles those natively saves you the evenings you currently spend in DMs. Software that does not leaves you to bolt them on.

## The benefits of tattoo booking software

The benefits fall on both sides of the booking.

**For the artist:**

- **Fewer no-shows.** A deposit paid before the time is held is the single most effective protection. Reminders help, but the deposit is what actually changes behaviour. See our guide to [reducing no-shows](/blog/reduce-no-shows-tattoo-studio/).
- **Less time chasing.** Size, placement, budget and reference images arrive in one request, so you are not piecing a brief together from twelve messages.
- **Bookings while you sleep.** Enquiries that land at 11pm or on a Sunday are captured, not lost to whoever replies first.
- **Records in one place.** Consent forms, deposits and past sessions sit with the client rather than across your phone, your inbox and a paper folder.

**For the client:**

- **One clear way to book.** A link, a short form, their references attached. No account to create.
- **Certainty.** They know the deposit, the policy and the date in writing, and get a confirmation by email.
- **Fewer surprises.** A reminder before the appointment, with anything they need to prepare.

## How to choose: five questions

1. **Where do your clients come from?** If it is Instagram and word of mouth, marketplace features are worth nothing to you; pick on cost and workflow. If a marketplace genuinely finds you clients, its commission is a cost of sale, not a fee.
2. **How many artists share the books?** Solo artists have the widest choice and the least reason to pay. Studios with a front desk need real staff calendars and should weight that heavily.
3. **How do you take deposits today?** If you are chasing bank transfers, the deposit flow is the feature to test first. Check where the money lands, when, and what the platform keeps. Our [tattoo deposit guide](/blog/tattoo-deposit-guide/) covers sensible amounts and policies.
4. **Where do your consent forms live?** If they are on paper, decide whether you want them digital and attached to the client. See our [tattoo consent form guide](/blog/tattoo-consent-form-guide/) for what a form should cover.
5. **What does the full monthly cost come to?** Add the subscription, the per-artist licences, the add-ons you need and a realistic estimate of marketplace commission. The headline price is rarely the real one.

## Red flags

- **A cut of every booking or deposit.** It grows as you get busier and punishes your best clients.
- **Clients must create an account to book.** Every extra step loses people who tapped your link on a phone.
- **Fixed short slots only.** If the software cannot hold a six-hour session cleanly, it was not built for you.
- **No way to try it with a real booking.** Test the full flow, deposit included, before you move anything.
- **Prices you cannot find.** If the real cost needs a sales call, expect it to be higher than the advert.

## Switching without losing bookings

1. **Keep your existing bookings where they are.** Let them run out on the old system rather than migrating them.
2. **Point new enquiries at the new link.** Change your Instagram bio link and your DM auto-reply first.
3. **Run one real booking end to end,** deposit and consent form included, before you announce anything.
4. **Export your client list** from the old system so you have it, then stop paying once the last old appointment is done.
